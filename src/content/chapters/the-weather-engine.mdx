Nature takes its turn before the gardener does. The **weather engine** is a small, pure function: it takes yesterday's weather and the garden, and returns the next garden plus a few sentences about what happened. It is deterministic – replaying the log always produces exactly the garden in `world.json`, and `npm run world:check` proves it on every pull request.

## Six conditions, checked in order

Open-Meteo reports daily values: rain, snow, sunshine hours, the night minimum, gusts. The engine reduces them to one condition – the first rule that matches wins:

## The stream widens, the sand banks grow

The stream runs through a trough near the east end of the garden. Its level goes from 0 (a dry bed) to 8 (brimming): rain raises it, sun lowers it. Because the trough is curved, a higher level is also a *wider* stream – and when the water sinks, sand banks appear on both sides. Frost freezes it; the ice melts on the first day above 5 °C.

## Storms and the paper boat

A storm blows away every blossom and every apple. If a tree is grown enough (45 % of its full size), the tallest one falls – at most one every 60 days. The fallen log stays in the garden: mushrooms can grow near it, and the gardener can build a **bridge** from it. No tree to fell? Then the storm strands a paper boat on the sand bank, and the next heavy rain carries it away.

## The plants and their rules

Every plant has planting months, a growing season, a place it likes and neighbours it needs – or avoids. Lavender needs full sun, ferns need shade, mushrooms need wood.
