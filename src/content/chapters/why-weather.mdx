Most generative art is generated by *someone*: a seed you pick, a parameter you tune, a button you press. **Grow** has a gardener, but the most important force in it is not under anyone's control. It is the weather in Heilbronn – the real one, measured, one day at a time.

## One day of sky, one choice, one commit

The garden lives in a public GitHub repository. Its whole state is one file, [`world/world.json`](https://github.com/Fluory/Grow/blob/main/world/world.json). Every morning at 08:53 a Claude routine asks [Open-Meteo](https://open-meteo.com/) what the weather was like yesterday at 49.14° N, 9.21° E. That day is reduced to one of six conditions, and the condition acts on the garden first:

- **Rain grows.** Every plant in its growing season grows one stage – two after a downpour – and the stream rises.
- **Sun ripens.** Blossoms open, apples ripen, the stream sinks and the sand banks grow.
- **Frost stops.** Nothing grows, the stream freezes, the sunflowers wilt.
- **Storm fells.** Blossoms blow away – and sometimes a grown tree comes down.

Only then does the gardener get a turn: plant one thing, build one thing, water one plant, harvest, or simply rest. The result lands on `main` as exactly one commit, with the weather in its title:

```text
Day 3: An oak sapling on the little hill (rain 4.2 mm)
Day 2: Watering the young pine (sun 9.8 h)
Day 1: A pine sapling in the west of the garden (sun 10.6 h)
Day 0: An empty garden bed by a stream
```

## Slow, and not up to us

A tree needs about a hundred stages to be fully grown. In Heilbronn that is roughly a year and a half of rain days. You cannot hurry it: watering adds a stage on a dry day, but only one, and only if the gardener chooses to spend the day on it. A dry summer will show in the garden. So will a wet one, and a cold snap in April.

<Callout icon="🌦️">
  The weather is recorded **as it was**. If the weather service is unreachable, the routine repeats the last known day and says so – in the commit, in the logbook and on the day page. It never invents a sunny day.
</Callout>

## Why this is worth watching

Because it is honest. Every change in the picture has a cause you can look up: *this* oak grew because it rained 12 mm on a Tuesday in May; *that* log is lying there because gusts of 72 km/h crossed the garden on the last day of July. The [weather record](/weather) is the garden's diary, and the [logbook](/logbook) is its chronicle.

Everything that shapes it is open: the rules, the renderer, the prompt the routine follows ([`ROUTINE.md`](https://github.com/Fluory/Grow/blob/main/ROUTINE.md)) and the complete history. The garden is transparently **AI-maintained**, and every daily commit says so.
